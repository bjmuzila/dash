No-wick, round 2: what a tolerance buys you

Wick ≤ 0%, 1%, 2%, 5%, 10% of bar range · ES & NQ · 1h / 4h / daily · 2017–2026 · 240 hypothesis tests

Relaxing the definition worked — daily is now testable. One result survived; the 1h story did not.

Loosening close == high to upper wick ≤ 5% of range takes the ES daily sample from 5 signals to 234. That was the point of the exercise, and it delivered. But testing 240 hypotheses means the bar for "real" rises sharply: a Bonferroni-corrected threshold on this data sits at |t| = 3.57, and nothing in the grid clears it.

The one candidate worth a second look is buying NQ after a daily down-bar that closed within 5% of its low — 62% win rate, profit factor 2.25, and it got stronger after 2022. The catch is in section 4, and it's a big one.

4 · The catch — is it a signal, or is it just NQ going up?

This is the test that matters, and it is the one most backtests skip. If buying dips in NQ works simply because NQ tripled over the sample, then the wick condition is decoration. So: compare the signal against buying every down day, and against buying every day.

NQ ES
Average points per trade, next-day open→close, costs included. Trade counts labelled on each bar.

The wick filter roughly triples the per-trade return on NQ — 18.6 pts for any down day, 58.8 pts when it closed on its low. That is a real gain in selectivity, and it is the single most encouraging number in this study. But note the two things sitting next to it: buying every down day already produces a t-stat of 2.68, and buying and holding every single day produces $404k against the signal's $124k. The base behaviour being exploited is NQ's upward drift. The wick condition concentrates that drift into fewer, better trades — it does not create an edge independent of it.

6 · Revised answer: how to trade this

  • The 1h no-wick idea is dead. It looked like a fade signal on the full sample at t = −2.80, but splitting at 2022 shows the sign reverses. That is the signature of a pattern fitted to one regime, not a behavioural constant. Drop it.
  • The daily NQ bear-fade is the only thing worth carrying forward — and as a filter for dip entries you were already going to consider, not as a standalone system. It fires about 11 times a year. At 62% and PF 2.25 with a $16k max drawdown it is respectable, but 103 trades cannot distinguish "edge" from "NQ went up a lot."
  • Do not run this on ES. Same rule on ES: t = 1.20 overall and 0.25 after 2022. The ES version is noise.
  • If you take it further, test it against the drift, not against zero. Every future variant should be benchmarked against buy-after-any-down-day, the way section 4 does. A rule that beats zero but not the drift has taught you nothing about wicks.
  • The highest-value next step is conditioning, not more geometry. You have GEX levels, TPO, value area, and IB stats in your dashboard already. "Down day closing on its low while below the zero-gamma flip" is a different hypothesis from "down day closing on its low," and it is the kind of thing that turns a 10pp gap into something that survives a holdout. The raw candle shape has now been tested about as far as 6.3M bars can take it.
