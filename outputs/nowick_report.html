Do no-wick candles predict continuation?

ES & NQ futures · strict close == high / close == low · 1h, 4h, daily · 24h Globex sessions · Apr 2017 – Jul 2026 · 6.3M one-minute bars

Verdict: no tradeable edge. Seven of eight rules lose money; the eighth is a coin flip.

The pattern is real and measurable — 1h no-wick candles show a statistically detectable mean-reversion tilt, the opposite of the usual continuation thesis. But the effect is roughly a third of a point on ES against a ±3-point noise band. Realistic slippage and commission eat it several times over. The daily timeframe cannot be tested at all: strict no-wick fires 8 times in nine years on ES.

1 · The trade rules, tested

Four rules per symbol on the 1h timeframe — the only tier with a real sample. Entry is always the open of the bar after the signal (no look-ahead). Costs charged at 0.60 points round trip: one tick of slippage each side plus commission. Continuation trades in the signal's direction; fade trades against it.

Not one rule clears zero with statistical confidence. The single non-negative result — NQ fade, exit next close, +$1,450 across nine years and 1,595 trades — has a t-stat of 0.08. That is a coin flip, not an edge.

3 · The signal is disappearing

How often a no-wick 1h candle occurs, by year. ES has fallen from 9.7% of all bars to 1.3%; NQ from 14.8% to 0.5%. Tick size relative to price, and vastly deeper books, mean a bar that closes exactly on its extreme is far rarer now. A large share of the historical sample comes from a market that no longer exists.

4 · They cluster overnight, not in the session

Signals concentrate in the thinnest hours of the Globex session — ES peaks at midnight ET. That points to a liquidity artifact rather than a behavioural pattern: in a thin book, a bar closing on its extreme means fewer participants, not more conviction.

6 · So how would you trade this?

Straight answer, given what the data shows:

  • Not as a standalone signal. The largest measured effect is ES bull at −0.33 points per bar. Your round-trip cost is roughly double that before slippage goes against you. There is no stop, target, or holding period in the tested grid that turns a −0.33 point tilt into a positive expectancy.
  • Not on the daily chart at all. Strict close == high produced 5 daily signals on ES and 11 on NQ across nine years. Any backtest you see on daily no-wicks is fitting noise, whoever produced it.
  • If you use it, use it as a filter, not a trigger. The one defensible reading is directional: a 1h close on the high is mildly bearish for the next bar, not bullish. That is worth knowing if you already have a setup and are deciding whether to take the long — it is not worth a trade by itself.
  • What would change the answer: loosening the definition to a tolerance (wick ≤ 2–5% of range) multiplies the sample by roughly 20× and makes daily testable. Adding context — volume, prior-bar relationship, position in the session range, distance from a GEX level — is where a marginal tilt sometimes becomes a real one. The raw geometric pattern on its own does not survive contact with costs.
